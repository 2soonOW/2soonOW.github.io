chapter vi - Reykjavík

2025-11-12

"Btw, i’m planning to go to Iceland in December.”

“You sure you’re going to send me photos? You better.”

It was 7 a.m., and an all-nighter of Overwatch had just ended. The faint blue glow from the monitor made it hard for him to keep his sleep deprived eyes open. But the moment she mentioned Iceland, something inside him had stirred.

Surely she’s going with her friends, he told himself. But deep down, he knew it wasn’t about Iceland at all. He just wanted to be wherever she was, in Iceland, in the same room while they gamed, or anywhere else the world might place her where he could follow her.

“Have you decided on a place?”

“Reykjavík,” she said.

He opened Google and typed it in, pretending not to notice that he’d misspelled it. Reykjavík, the search bar corrected him. Photos of snow-covered streets and the northern lights filled his screen. He hadn’t heard much about the city before, but now that he had, he knew it wasn’t a name he would ever forget.

“This looks amazing,” he said. “Have you decided on a hotel?”

“Yes,” she replied, and shared a faint itinerary, nothing too detailed, but enough to make his morning feel brighter than it had in weeks.

He thought Iceland sounded like a dream. He’d always loved snow. And he knew she loved snow, and that she loved snowy mountains, the kind that looked like they belonged to another world. Antarctica was her dream place after all. How could he not fall in love with someone who dreamed about going to the most mysterious place in the world?

For a moment, he was more excited about her trip than she was. He started imagining the two of them sharing stories about Reykjavík, as though he could live it through her, see through her eyes what he could never see in person. It was around Christmas, and Christmas is the prettiest of seasons. But in reality, he was really just happy that she was finally able to go to one of the places she had always talked about.

Maybe that is the strange thing about love, how it teaches you to live off imagination when reality fails to feed you.

And obviously, just like every sad story, this one also has a similar ending to it. The photos never came. Still, he couldn’t stop thinking about Reykjavík. In his mind, he had already traced the pale skies she must have seen.

“One day,” he told himself.

He decided he would go too, to see the same skies she saw and to perhaps feel a little of what she had felt there.

Reykjavík had become his dream too.
