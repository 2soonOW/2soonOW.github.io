Ambivalence

2026-03-02

Writing, Music, and ella

Why do I like writing about her? The simple answer would be that it’s the easiest way to move on and to have my thoughts spoken out loud. The complicated answer would be that I believe in creating rather than consuming, though I often fail to live up to my own beliefs. I believe we humans are designed to create, but in a world where all we seem to do is overly consume, writing feels like a lost art. With writing, you can express so many things on a piece of paper, which removes the impediment of having words left unsaid.

The real answer is a bit of both. I think it’s beautiful to write about her. I absolutely adore her. Writing about her gives me the freedom to create and dive deep into my imagination and process all these untouched thoughts without any restraint. It allows me to process grief in my own way.

Similarly, whenever I hear a song, my first instinct is to think of her. It scarcely matters what kind of song it is – melancholic or happy. The very first direction my mind wanders is toward her. I have long believed that music is the great unifier of the world; it always has been. Some of the biggest movements in the world have been orchestrated by music. And my personal battle within is no less than any real battle, or so my heart believes.

But really, don’t take my heart’s word for it. It has lived in denial and solitary for too long, and it tends to chirp fervent opinions about most things. So yes, many songs make me think about her, but naturally there are a few that stand out and resonate with me on a deeper level. I will be writing about three instrumental pieces that I am very much in love with at the moment, and each of them carry a profound meaning to them, giving some sort of meaning to my life.

Fairytail
by Phoria  

Phoria is a rather obscure artist whom I can almost guarantee you’ve never listened to. The violins that open Fairytail create imaginary landscapes brimming with fantastical emotion, just as a fairytale would. Just like in my own fairytale world, the music evokes the sense of someone dreaming of a better time. I have this entire different world in my head where nothing went wrong. Boy meets girl online, boy likes girl. The boy then lives happily ever after. And for a moment, while the music flows, I allow myself to be consumed in that possibility, even if only inside my head. If only life were as simple as a fairytale, no? Without the universe grinning back at you.

But the repercussions of that fantasy are very real. My life has become isolated. I cannot find peace, and it has affected my ability to complete my PR or find love, as I usually spend all my time lost in depression and far from reality. All I know is that a thousand fairy tales couldn’t fill the hole she left behind. 

You are allowed to pour all the reality you can into a fairytale, but you have to stick to the plot.

Ultimately, Fairytail is a classical song that utilizes an incredible orchestration of strings, whose violins move me through a landscape of peace and freedom and open the space for my own fairy tale world, a world full of yearning and longing for a girl I could dream about for an eternity. The girl who doesn’t exist anywhere but only in the grip of my imagination.

On the Nature of Daylight
By Max Ritcher

I’ve been slightly obsessed with Max Richter for the past couple of years. I usually listen to instrumental music while I work, and for a long time now that has mostly meant his compositions, along with some other greats like the late Jóhann Jóhannsson, Ludovico Einaudi, and the exemplary Ólafur Arnalds.

Anyone who listens to classical music, even if they don’t watch classical films, should probably know about Max Richter, or at least his most well-known piece, On the Nature of Daylight.  

What makes On the Nature of Daylight so special, in my opinion, is how deeply it reaches into your heart. It’s melancholic but still carries a sense of hope. When I listen to it while I’m sad (which is all the time), it doesn’t drag me down further; instead, it lets me sit with that sadness, process everything, while reminding me that there’s light somewhere behind it.

Richter often straddles the line between classical tradition and contemporary minimalism. Many classical enthusiasts appreciate his work because of his formal training and the care he brings to orchestration. Of course, some purists might find his style a little too cinematic or crossover for their taste, but overall he remains widely respected, and for good reason.

In contrast to the world this music takes me into, my real world often feels like a story of finality and a world where all roads seem to lead to sorrow and helplessness, no matter how hard I try to steer them in another direction. Listening to On the Nature of Daylight offers a different perspective. It allows me to open up a little more, to step outside the usual contemporary “ella” noise in my mind and see things with a bit more space and clarity.

As a bonus recommendation, I’d also suggest Recomposed by Max Richter: Vivaldi – The Four Seasons. It’s taking Vivaldi’s ideas and using them in a minimalistic style, which is just super interesting to me personally.

Adagio For Strings
By Samuel Barber, Leonard Bernstein, New York Philharmonic

I'm no classical music expert but for me, this is one of the greatest pieces on earth. It is gorgeous, songful and somber. I always listen to this piece when my emotions overwhelm me. Just listening to it makes me weep instantly; it is truly a powerful testament to the brilliance of music. The Laurens Symfonisch choir version of this actually changed my perspective of classical music. My deepest regret is not learning classical music at an earlier age as I think I would have enjoyed it so much and celebrated it by understanding it the way it’s meant to be listened to.
