chapter iii - sullen people

2025-11-07


I wonder if life experiences might give insight into how and why people act as they do. On the surface, it may not make a lot of sense to those who have never experienced the gut-wrenching reality of being unwanted or discarded. Because these things damage your internal logic in all areas of your life, making it difficult to find reason within your motivations. You start replaying conversations in your head like they meant something permanent, like they were some undeniable proof of a bond strong enough to survive any distance. 

Sullen people are not necessarily depressed in the traditional way of things. Of course, beneath the surface lies a persistent dullness, a different sort of fatigue that no sleep can fix. It’s a heaviness born from disappointment and slowly accumulates not from one great tragedy, but from a series of defeats. They once believed in things, and they just don’t anymore. It’s as simple as that. I believe there’s a certain cloud of darkness that surrounds them. 

But darkness has its own advantages, no? 

When our visual sense is muted, we’re compelled to navigate our surroundings through our other senses. It creates some space to tune into the inner workings of our own bodies. Many of us fear the dark because it represents the unknown, which is both within and around us, moulded subtly by the absence of sight. Yet in avoiding darkness, we risk becoming trapped in the illusions of visual perception, which is often biased and prone to the brain’s tendency to fill in missing details with assumptions. But when the noise of the world fades, and the quiet starts to congregate slowly into our conscience, we realize how much of our identity has been constructed by what we can see, and how little we’ve trusted what we can feel.
