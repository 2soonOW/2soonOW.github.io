2026-02-18

A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms is a reminder of why fantasy is such a perennially popular genre i.e. to escape from the horrors of the real world into a world where the horrors haven’t chased your escapism. It has revived the spirit of that 19 year old boy who used to wake up early on Monday mornings and avoid social media at all costs, just to escape spoilers for his favorite show on television. If I still haven’t convinced anyone that this is the greatest show on television since Game of Thrones, the Dunk and Egg stargazing scene alone should suffice. It's the exact kind of human moment that has been missing after all the dark and gritty memories of both ASOIAF and HOTD. This show is so peak and the fact that one of the shortest novella in the canon is being adapted with such care feels like justice not just to the story, but to the entire ASOIAF legacy.

One of the best characters in AKOTSK is Maekar. He may, in fact, be one of the most compelling figures in the entire book. I also like to believe that I love his character even more because, on a personal level, I understand something about guilt. The complexity of such characters is exactly why these are the best fantasy books ever written.

“And my father ... he never thought the throne would pass to him, and yet it did. He used to say that was his punishment for the blow that slew his brother. I pray he found the peace in death that he never knew in life.” - Aemon Targaryen

So let me make one thing clear: Maekar Targaryen never intended to harm Baelor, despite the rumors that suggest otherwise. It is mostly because tons of people these days find it hard to grasp nuance. The idea that someone could fight against someone (his own brother in this scenario) and use deadly force, yet still love them and not intend to kill them, requires a certain level of understanding that not everything is morally “good” or “bad.” Maekar was torn between his own son and his brother. Was Aerion an awful Targaryen? Yes. Did Maekar still love him as a son and desperately want to defend him from also being attacked and potentially killed? Yes. hence why he would fight so fiercely against Baelor to get to him. A parent usually doesn’t stop loving their child just because they do some bad things, even if an outsider would hate the kid for said bad things. 

For a lot of new viewers and readers who just got into GGRM's universe, this could be a hard concept to grasp. Martin’s characters are, more often than not, steeped in tragedy and Maekar Targaryen is no exception.

After the death of Baelor, Maekar is struck by grief so heavy, he could never recover from it. Maekar always bitterly regretted Baelor's passing and marked its anniversary every year. His devotion to hierarchy and to the sanctity of law left no room for softness as a king, but what do you do when you do not want to bear the crown and yet have it forced upon you? Did he love his brother? Almost certainly. But Maekar didn’t really know how to grieve. And so his mourning was private and the real tragedy is not simply that he killed his brother, but that he could never forgive himself for the rest of his life for what he did, despite it not being directly his fault.

I recently had an argument with a friend about Daenerys’ character and the possibility of her being completely coerced into her father’s shadow i.e, becoming the Mad Queen herself. Metaphorically, it felt like witnessing the assassination of a long-running character arc: Daenerys shifted from a noble paladin, a symbol of hope, into the ultimate evil to be defeated by the “real hero,” Jon, undoing everything the show and books had carefully built over years.

I will be presenting a little analysis explaining why I believe the showrunners and perhaps the general audience, completely mishandled the direction her story was taking. My observations are based on my understanding of the show and Daenerys’ character arc, and if I get any details wrong, I’m more than open to being humbled.

The “Mad Queen” thesis, as retroactively imposed upon Daenerys, is intellectually untenable when examined within the moral architecture of Game of Thrones. Daenerys Targaryen had not, prior to the destruction of King’s Landing, committed acts more egregious than most of the celebrated characters. Executing the slavers? Conquering a city to liberate the slaves? Burning a person who betrayed her (Varys) when she had promised to do exactly that if he ever did? In the context of Game of Thrones, none of that is truly horrible or worse than any other “crimes” committed in open war. She actually had potential to be a lot more cruel than she was in many situations but never did because she isn’t evil and she does not enjoy suffering. The showrunners posthumously painting Daenerys as having been inherently villainous all along simply doesn’t work.

I think Dany being a villain is entirely dependent on the context. For the slavers of Slavers Bay, she's a villain. For the slaves, she's a hero. Which side has the moral high ground? it should be fairly obvious. To Cersei, she's a villain. To Jon, she's pretty much a hero. Once again, which character probably has the right of it? To a modern audience, we perceive Daenerys as a hero because she believes in the ideals of freedom, she shows mercy when possible, she defends those who cannot defend themselves, and she brings people together. The characters in the show who are aligned with the "good" side of the moral spectrum all see Daenarys as a hero because she believes in those ideals. Sure, you can argue that Daenarys is a villain, but you would be arguing from the point of view of someone who has some very questionable morals.

They spent seven seasons pondering over the fact that Daenerys was the hero, the epitome of goodness, showing a character with flaws but in spite of those flaws, ultimately always did the right thing. Daenerys had been presented as an idealist, a woman on a crusade to make the world a better place, not a mad zealot. Daenerys and Jon Snow were perhaps the only characters always acting in the interest of other people, helping, saving, and rescuing them even when they were loathed for their choices. And they didn’t just do it for their people, nor for “the realm” that Varys claims to serve. No, Daenerys and Jon defended all people, even those they weren’t responsible for. For example, Is Robb Stark a villain? he did raid through the Westerlands, which certainly would have caused death, starvation, and ruin. Ofcourse, we don't see it that way because we think Robb is justified. As for Daenarys' war on Slaver's Bay, slavery is an abhorrent practice that causes thousands to starve and die anyway. Isn't that justified? Especially by Westerosi standards? What do you think Ned, Robb, and Jon would have done if they were in that situation? People would be calling Dany a villain if she simply bought the Unsullied and left, despite seeing firsthand the cruelty and evilness of slavery. They would call her selfish in her desire for power, and unwilling to care about the suffering of common people.

And even if I don't ride the moral high ground for Dany saying that she is way too power hungry, which is the big one for most people. Like up until this point, Dany essentially does what most of the audience believes to be morally right. And now she's executing defenceless prisoners, something which we don't identify with. I think this is the event that caused a lot of people to turn against Daenarys. The Tarly’s certainly didn't deserve to get roasted, but executing prisoners of war is a common practice in Westeros. Even Stannis, mind you(the greatest followers of laws and duty in all of Westeros) had a habit of doing it as well.

Daenerys goes to war with the cities of Slaver’s Bay, delaying her grand plan to claim her birthright in Westeros over people that shouldn’t be her concern. She takes one look at slavery and knows it is wrong. She knows she has the power to overthrow it, to change things, so she does because she knows no one else will, no matter the cost to her, no matter that ruling Essos isn’t her goal. After all, she has been a slave herself, sold and used like a piece of jewelry by her brother Viserys and Illyrio Mopatis to Khal Drogo. When she crowns herself Queen of Meereen, it’s not because she craves power. It’s to establish a fairer life for the people, to oversee the liberation of the slaves and the new class hierarchy that it creates. Despite being young and inexperienced, Daenerys was a good enough queen, if not always a good strategist. She cared. She never did anything on a whim, for her own gain, or out of selfishness. She surely never shied away from doing things that didn’t please her. 

When Daenerys tells Jon, “I don’t have love here, I only have fear,” the implication that she would completely unravel over a lack of popularity is absurd. She had already endured far worse and continued on her path. Watching all of Westeros doubt her, conspire against her, and maneuver to put Jon on the Iron Throne was painful precisely because Daenerys had done nothing wrong except be Aerys’ daughter. Somehow, Jon’s Targaryen lineage raises no similar concern. Varys noticed her isolating herself, mourning her closest allies, refusing to eat, and yet no one attempted to support her. Instead, they concluded she was “going mad” and that nothing could be done.

The most insulting choice, however, was making Jon’s romantic rejection the “final straw” that drives her to destroy King’s Landing. Suggesting that a woman would abandon all empathy and choose tyranny and mass murder because of unrequited love is perhaps Game of Thrones’ lowest narrative point. By framing Daenerys as power-hungry and sexually frustrated, HBO reinforces a deeply patriarchal idea: that women in power are prone to irrational, violent fury. Her descent mirrors Cersei’s destruction of the Sept of Baelor—another independent, sexually liberated woman who “goes mad”—as if the story is insisting that power inherently corrupts fierce women.

Some argue Sansa’s coronation counters this, but it does not. Sansa introduces no revolutionary change; she demands only the North’s independence, granted immediately by her brother on the Iron Throne. She restores a rigid, patriarchal vision of Westerosi life, standing for neither diversity nor justice for the oppressed, and is shown to fear and disdain those who are different. If her rise is seen as feminist, it is an illusion. It is pandering both Benioff and Weiss patting themselves on the back for crowning a woman, immediately after betraying another powerful woman who had been stabbed in the narrative by her lover during a passionate embrace.

To communicate in clear terms that she is the ultimate evil who must be destroyed, the finale employs visual imagery inspired by historical footage of dictators such as Adolf Hitler and Benito Mussolini for the scene where Daenerys addresses her troops. An entire separate op-ed could be written about the gravity of this, but to put a woman who stood for freedom and unity on the same level as these Nazi and fascist dictators is not only terribly insensitive and inconsiderate, it’s also dangerous, and a spit on the face of all the people who were deeply invested into her character.

Why do I like writing about her? The simple answer would be that it’s the easiest way to move on and to have my thoughts spoken out loud. The complicated answer would be that I believe in creating rather than consuming, though I often fail to live up to my own beliefs. I believe we humans are designed to create, but in a world where all we seem to do is overly consume, writing feels like a lost art. With writing, you can express so many things on a piece of paper, which removes the impediment of having words left unsaid.

The real answer is a bit of both. I think it’s beautiful to write about her. I absolutely adore her. Writing about her gives me the freedom to create and dive deep into my imagination and process all these untouched thoughts without any restraint. It allows me to process grief in my own way.

Similarly, whenever I hear a song, my first instinct is to think of her. It scarcely matters what kind of song it is – melancholic or happy. The very first direction my mind wanders is toward her. I have long believed that music is the great unifier of the world; it always has been. Some of the biggest movements in the world have been orchestrated by music. And my personal battle within is no less than any real battle, or so my heart believes.

But really, don’t take my heart’s word for it. It has lived in denial and solitary for too long, and it tends to chirp fervent opinions about most things. So yes, many songs make me think about her, but naturally there are a few that stand out and resonate with me on a deeper level. I will be writing about three instrumental pieces that I am very much in love with at the moment, and each of them carry a profound meaning to them, giving some sort of meaning to my life.

Fairytail
by Phoria  

Phoria is a rather obscure artist whom I can almost guarantee you’ve never listened to. The violins that open Fairytail create imaginary landscapes brimming with fantastical emotion, just as a fairytale would. Just like in my own fairytale world, the music evokes the sense of someone dreaming of a better time. I have this entire different world in my head where nothing went wrong. Boy meets girl online, boy likes girl. The boy then lives happily ever after. And for a moment, while the music flows, I allow myself to be consumed in that possibility, even if only inside my head. If only life were as simple as a fairytale, no? Without the universe grinning back at you.

But the repercussions of that fantasy are very real. My life has become isolated. I cannot find peace, and it has affected my ability to complete my PR or find love, as I usually spend all my time lost in depression and far from reality. All I know is that a thousand fairy tales couldn’t fill the hole she left behind. 

You are allowed to pour all the reality you can into a fairytale, but you have to stick to the plot.

Ultimately, Fairytail is a classical song that utilizes an incredible orchestration of strings, whose violins move me through a landscape of peace and freedom and open the space for my own fairy tale world, a world full of yearning and longing for a girl I could dream about for an eternity. The girl who doesn’t exist anywhere but only in the grip of my imagination.

On the Nature of Daylight
By Max Ritcher

I’ve been slightly obsessed with Max Richter for the past couple of years. I usually listen to instrumental music while I work, and for a long time now that has mostly meant his compositions, along with some other greats like the late Jóhann Jóhannsson, Ludovico Einaudi, and the exemplary Ólafur Arnalds.

Anyone who listens to classical music, even if they don’t watch classical films, should probably know about Max Richter, or at least his most well-known piece, On the Nature of Daylight.  

What makes On the Nature of Daylight so special, in my opinion, is how deeply it reaches into your heart. It’s melancholic but still carries a sense of hope. When I listen to it while I'm sad (which is all the time), it doesn’t drag me down further; instead, it lets me sit with that sadness, process everything, while reminding me that there’s light somewhere behind it.

Richter often straddles the line between classical tradition and contemporary minimalism. Many classical enthusiasts appreciate his work because of his formal training and the care he brings to orchestration. Of course, some purists might find his style a little too cinematic or crossover for their taste, but overall he remains widely respected, and for good reason.

In contrast to the world this music takes me into, my real world often feels like a story of finality and a world where all roads seem to lead to sorrow and helplessness, no matter how hard I try to steer them in another direction. Listening to On the Nature of Daylight offers a different perspective. It allows me to open up a little more, to step outside the usual contemporary “ella” noise in my mind and see things with a bit more space and clarity.

As a bonus recommendation, I’d also suggest Recomposed by Max Richter: Vivaldi – The Four Seasons. It’s taking Vivaldi's ideas and using them in a minimalistic style, which is just super interesting to me personally.

Adagio For Strings
By Samuel Barber, Leonard Bernstein, New York Philharmonic

For me, this is one of the greatest pieces on earth. It is gorgeous, songful and somber. I always listen to this piece when my emotions overwhelm me. Just listening to it makes me weep instantly; it is truly a powerful testament to the brilliance of music. The Laurens Symfonisch choir version of this actually changed my perspective of classical music. My deepest regret is not learning classical music at an earlier age as I think I would have enjoyed it so much and celebrated it by understanding it the way it's meant to be listened to.

I have this weird idea in my head of something called “Shoegaze Life.” It’s basically my real life, evocative of the same mood that music from bands like MBV, Beach House, and Mazzy Star etc create. Of course, real life lacks that dreamy filter, but it still has the character. You brush past so many people every day. Some you may never know anything about, but others might become your friend someday. What a difference a day makes, no? On December of '21, I encountered someone who would go on to change my life. And that day was indeed special. Just another regular day for her, but a chance of a lifetime for me.

The girl could have been nothing special, really. She could have just been a projection of my imagination. But I disagree. She was the most special'est'(i know its not a word) person I have ever met.

Think about it this way:

The current modern city living jades even the most tender of lovers. Sentimentality is always accompanied by a lurking sardonicism, just in case. There are signs of love everywhere, because you can’t get hurt by every stranger you encounter. Yet the need persists to express these signs of love. You can’t pass up any potential paramour either; there’s no guarantee you’ll ever encounter them again.

Of course, I don’t want to send myself into a state of euphoric panic and ignore the realities of life, which are far more than just a pretty shoegaze filter over my lenses. To me, nothing feels lonelier than a world full of other people, and coping with that loneliness is a dangerous game. I refuse to believe something this beautiful happens by chance, my ordinary life turning into something unforgettable through a fleeting connection with her. 

Through meeting ella. 

But in a world of billions of people, there are a billion better men than I am. A billion men she would pick over me. So my best chance is to dream of her. Now I dream of her so often, and it always leaves me with a lingering, but not so cruel, sadness that never quite goes away.

PSG vs Bayern Munich, as a football fan, was the most insane game I have ever witnessed, and if ten years ago you had told me that PSG was capable of playing a game like this, I would have laughed my ass off or at least told you that was a lot to be skeptical about.

Alan Shearer, who was commentating on the game said: “It’s one of the greatest games I’ve ever been to. It’s been a pleasure and a privilege to be here tonight.” This was all the football fans at the moment.

I mean, all the ingredients were there, and somehow it still managed to exceed every expectation i had with this game. In terms of stylish, high-tempo, technically brilliant football, the first half was as good as the sport gets.

Both sides played with complete freedom and ambition. There were five goals scored, as many as have ever been in a first half of a Champions League semi-final, and yet it could have been even more. Clear cut chances came and went as Dembele, Kvaratskhelia, and Doue, supported by the ever inventive Vitinha and relentlessly attacking Nuno and Hakimi, tore into Bayern’s defenses.

You simply could not take your eyes off it for a second. Chances were being missed even as broadcasters replayed moments from just seconds earlier.

Kvaratskhelia dropping one of the greatest CL performances of all time in the matter of 70 minutes has to be one of the most pivotal moments of any football game in recent history. And there are only a few better than him at exploiting these uneasy situations, teasing defenders who are already scrambling for position and punishing the slightest opening they concede.

He did it for his first goal, which settled PSG after falling behind to Kane’s penalty. He did it again for his second at 5–2, which at the time felt like it had put the tie beyond Bayern.

I want to address the biggest elephant in the room and speak honestly about the strange anhedonic tendencies that exist within me or perhaps even more accurately, the version of myself I perceive whenever I think about her. To put this plainly: I am painfully obsessed with ella. Yet at the same time, I remain acutely self aware of the emotional compulsion and  excesses that have led me into this perpetual state of madness. 

Obsession - Firstly and very clearly, I do not view my obsession as harmful to her. That distinction matters to me. At no point, in no conceivable reality, would I ever physically, emotionally, or virtually harass or assault her. I have left her alone as she wanted me to do.  My fixation exists almost entirely within the architecture of my own mind. But is it harmful to me? That is not a very long debate; the answer is unequivocally yes. To become consumed by this idea of someone and  to exist in a constant state of yearning and unrequited longing is very psychologically corrosive.

Delusion -  To create art, I have to become delusional. I do not mean that in a performative or melodramatic sense. The only reason I can bring even the vaguest of my thoughts to life is because I have the ability to imagine scenarios far beyond anyone’s imagination. My head erupts with ideas whenever I think about ella. She has become my creative catalyst, unironically.

If the words of my seventh grade teacher were anything to go by ; naqui is only productive when he genuinely cares about something ; then perhaps this obsession was inevitable, because genuine interest is exceedingly rare for me. Most things leave me emotionally inert. ella obviously did not.

Psychologically, I think fixation operates partially through idealization. I love this little piece I read from Sigmund Freud talking about something called cathexis, where he basically states that emotional energy becomes invested in a person or idea until they cease being merely human and instead become symbolic. I do not think I am obsessed solely with ella as a person. I think I am obsessed with what she represents inside my mind: a constant state of loneliness. Then there is limerence: my involuntary state of obsessive romantic desire, fueled less by reality and more by fantasy and, I am pretty sure, emotional deprivation. My brain has a terrifying tendency to romanticize what it cannot fully possess. And the irony is almost embarrassing.

I constantly say, “nothing compares to ella,” yet ella herself has never truly done anything special for me. Heck, she wasn’t even there during my saddest moments, though it is arguable that most of those moments were caused by her absence. I do not think I played an important role in her life. Most of what I feel was constructed internally. There exists a common belief that I fixate on her because I am attempting to compensate for past wounds or failed relationships. Perhaps there is some truth to that. But mostly, I do not think so. Experience undeniably shapes attachment, but I do not believe my past relationships directly caused this. If anything, ella merely amplified something that was already dormant within me.

Instead of seeking relationships grounded in reciprocity, I continue living alongside painful memories and imagined possibilities, completely taking a U turn away from emotional reality, which is IMO what a person truly needs. In doing so, I deprive myself of connections that might actually bring peace rather than this inevitable heartache.

Yet despite understanding all of this intellectually, the feeling remains unchanged. I WANT to miss her. I need to miss her to be the man I am right now. That is the most frightening part.

Dreams - I see her almost every night in my dreams. At some point, you begin questioning whether this level of fixation is psychologically normal. I have an extraordinarily difficult time sleeping because of it. I wake up with an ache in my chest that is almost inexplicable. And these dreams are never grotesque or nightmarish. They are painfully beautiful. Really, really beautiful.

I have lived through countless imagined moments with her. Scenarios so vivid and emotionally layered that they just linger in my mind long after I am awake. Dreams where we reconciled our shortcomings. Dreams where we spoke honestly. Dreams where she literally gave me a house tour. Even within the dream itself, I feel butterflies in my chest. Sometimes I wake up disoriented, momentarily mourning the realization that none of it actually happened. The pain is sometimes too much to take.

What makes this obsession so dangerous is the mind’s inability to properly distinguish emotional reality from physical reality. As strange as it sounds, ella feels as real to me as any person possibly could. The fact that our relationship existed exclusively online does not diminish that reality in my mind. Human attachment has never depended solely on physical proximity. People fall in love with voices, ideas, presences, fragments of personality, relatable traits, literally all sorts of things. The internet merely creates a new common ground through which longing can manifest itself.

Maybe that is why the dreams feel so devastatingly authentic. She was someone important in my life, and my brain perceives her as absent rather than nonexistent. And absence is far more haunting than fiction.

Self realization - I understand, intellectually, that much of this obsession has been constructed internally. I know what projection is. I know what idealization is. I understand that loneliness can transform admiration into emotional dependency. More often than not, this self-realization simply allows me to observe myself deteriorating in real time with absolute clarity. Yet despite possessing the vocabulary to explain my condition, I remain unable to escape it. I have deliberately chosen it.

I think, deep down, I am more terrified of emotional emptiness than emotional pain. Because pain, at the very least, proves that something inside me is still capable of feeling intensely.

Without her, I sometimes fear that I would return to the emotional numbness that existed before I met her, which I believe has already started to build inside me. That life I hated so much. Despite how destructive this fixation has become, there is still a part of me that prefers this catastrophic feeling over that life, regardless of how slowly and painfully it feels as though my skin is being peeled away.
